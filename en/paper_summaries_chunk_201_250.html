Extrinsic calibration of a camera and laser range finder (improves camera calibration)

https://doi.org/10.1109/iros.2004.1389752
This paper is an early key paper that practically formalizes extrinsic calibration between a camera and a 2D laser range finder using the checkerboard plane as a common reference.
01
Background
In mobile robot perception using both a camera and a laser, accurately aligning the two sensor coordinate systems was the basic condition for3D recognition and distance projection.
02
Problem
The paper deals with how to estimate the rigid transform between a camera without natural point correspondence and a2D laser range finder.
03
Prior limitations
Existing methods rely on manual measurement or independent correction for each sensor, resulting in low reproducibility and difficulty in reducing uncertainty in camera intrinsic estimation.
04
Goal
The goal is to calculate the external calibration of the two sensors using only planar checkerboard observations and show that this constraint also helps with camera calibration.
05
Method
The method combines a direct solution that minimizes algebraic error and nonlinear refinement based on reprojection error by having a camera and a laser simultaneously observe a checkerboard in various poses.
06
Core idea
The key idea is that the plane intersection line obtained from a laser scan and the plane correction information of the camera image can be linked to each other with the constraint that they share the same physical plane.
07
Validation
Verification was conducted through theoretical development and actual camera-laser platform experiments, and is more of a sensor calibration experiment than a robot driving dataset.
08
Results
The results present the first published calibration tool-level procedure for this problem and show that camera intrinsic parameter variance can be reduced.
09
Comparison
The comparison focuses on the fact that the checkerboard-based common constraints method provides a more systematic starting point for sensor fusion compared to separate calibration or manual alignment.
10
Significance
The significance is that it clarifies the standard problem of cross-modal geometric calibration, which is repeatedly required in subsequent LiDAR-camera calibration, RGB-D mapping, and mobile manipulation.
11
Limitations
Limitations include that performance is highly dependent on2D laser and planar target visibility, sufficiently diverse target poses, and accurate corner and laser-line extraction.
12
Future work
Future tasks include outlier-robust estimation, targetless calibration, and expansion to more complex sensor combinations such as3D LiDAR and rolling-shutter cameras.

The Gaussian Sampling Strategy for Probabilistic Roadmap Planners

V. Boor; M.H. Overmars; A. Frank van der Stappen
https://doi.org/10.1109/robot.1999.772447
Gaussian sampler is a classic paper that improves the performance of PRM's narrow passage search with a simple sampling rule that better captures the area around the obstacle boundary.
01
Background
The background was a time when sampling-based motion planning emerged as a practical solution in high-dimensional configuration space, but performance was determined by where the samples were distributed.
02
Problem
The problem is that when PRM randomly covers the entire free space, it does not sufficiently sample obstacle boundaries and narrow passages that determine the actual path.
03
Prior limitations
The existing limitation was that uniform sampling wastes samples in a wide free space and misses narrow connections, increasing the probability of roadmap connection failure.
04
Goal
The goal is to present a simple sampling strategy that provides better free-space coverage and connectivity without significantly changing the existing PRM structure.
05
Method
The method picks a point and its surrounding Gaussian perturbation points and adopts the free point as a roadmap sample when one side is collision and the other side is free.
06
Core idea
The core idea is to automatically oversample narrow passage entrances and the vicinity of surfaces by taking advantage of the fact that free/occupied pairs frequently occur near obstacle boundaries.
07
Validation
Verification corresponds to offline simulation evaluation compared to uniform sampling type PRM on a geometric path-planning benchmark.
08
Results
The result is that Gaussian sampling increases the possibility of connection with fewer samples in scenes with difficult passages and improves the practical success rate of PRM.
09
Comparison
Comparison has the advantage of being boundary-sensitive over the uniform strategy, but all samples may be concentrated near the boundary, so a balance with wide free-space coverage is necessary.
10
Significance
The significance is that it provided an initial basis for why later obstacle-based PRM and bridge-test samplers should be aimed at narrow passages.
11
Limitations
The limitation is that it does not address dynamics, uncertainty, and real robot execution and is tied to the planning-level assumption that the collision checker and environmental model are accurate.
12
Future work
Future tasks are to more actively reflect the problem structure, such as adaptive mixture sampling, learning-based sampler, and kinodynamic planning.

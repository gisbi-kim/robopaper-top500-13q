ICRA · 1999

Monte Carlo Localization for Mobile Robots

https://doi.org/10.1109/robot.1999.772544
This is a classic paper that deals with global position estimation, position tracking, and kidnapped robot situations in one probability frame by maintaining the multi-hypothesis distribution of the robot position with a particle filter.
01
Background
In mobile robot navigation, even if there is a map, location estimation was a key bottleneck in mission success due to initial location uncertainty and sensor noise.
02
Problem
The paper addresses the problem of estimating the posterior probability of a robot's posture on a map in real time by receiving distance sensor observations and movement commands as input.
03
Prior limitations
The existing Kalman series location estimation has a strong unimodal Gaussian assumption, making it difficult to express situations where multiple candidate locations coexist, such as global initialization or kidnapped robots.
04
Goal
The goal is to present a localization algorithm that handles global localization, local tracking, and relocalization in a probabilistic and computable manner.
05
Method
The method expresses the robot posture hypothesis as particles, predicts samples with a motion model, updates weights with sensor likelihood, and maintains the distribution through resampling.
06
Core idea
The core idea is to naturally absorb nonlinearity, multimodality, and rapid position loss by approximating the posterior with a set of samples rather than a closed-form distribution.
07
Validation
Verification was centered on global localization and tracking experiments using an indoor mobile robot's occupancy/grid map and actual sensor logs.
08
Results
The results showed that when the number of particles is sufficient, position convergence and tracking are possible even in a complex indoor environment, and re-convergence is possible even in a kidnapped robot situation.
09
Comparison
The comparison criteria are the expression limits of Markov localization and Kalman-style estimation, and the Monte Carlo method showed strengths in uncertainty expression and resilience rather than accuracy.
10
Significance
The significance is that it is a practical starting point for stochastic robotics that led to the standard 2D localization pipeline of the ROS era, including AMCL.
11
Limitations
The limitations are that it is sensitive to map and sensor model quality, and if the number of particles is insufficient, it may lose small modes in the posterior and miss global convergence.
12
Future work
Future tasks naturally led to adaptive sampling, stronger sensor models, and expansion into dynamic environments and 3D sensors.

IJRR · 2015

Keyframe-based visual-inertial odometry using nonlinear optimization

https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364914554813
OKVIS is a representative VIO system that optimizes both camera reprojection error and IMU error through keyframe-based sliding-window nonlinear optimization.
01
Background
Small flying robots and mobile robots had to use rich geometric information from cameras and high-frequency motion information from IMUs in environments where GPS was weak.
02
Problem
The problem is to estimate the metric pose, velocity, bias, and sparse landmark state in real time by receiving image features and IMU measurements as input.
03
Prior limitations
Existing filter-based VIO was sensitive to linearization and marginalization orders, and pure vision had limitations that became unstable in scale and fast motion.
04
Goal
The goal is to create a general-purpose visual-inertial odometry that satisfies both accuracy and real-time by combining keyframe selection and nonlinear optimization.
05
Method
The method inserts the reprojection residual, IMU residual, and landmark parameters into a sliding window factor graph and marginalizes the old state.
06
Core idea
The core idea is to preserve the tight coupling of IMU and vision while limiting the optimization size by maintaining a keyframe with high information content without using all frames.
07
Validation
Verification was performed by comparing trajectory error and runtime in indoor and outdoor camera-IMU data and MAV series experiments.
08
Results
The results demonstrated an optimization-based VIO capable of real-time operation with greater accuracy against drift and sudden motion than the filter-based approach.
09
Comparison
The comparison is based on MSCKF-type filters and vision-only odometry, and although the amount of calculation increases compared to a strong baseline, it shows advantages in accuracy and consistency.
10
Significance
The significance is that it became a key reference point for the keyframe plus marginalization design shared by subsequent VINS, BASALT, and factor-graph VIO.
11
Limitations
Limitations are that it is sensitive to feature tracking quality, rolling shutter, time synchronization, and calibration errors, and does not provide dense/semantic maps.
12
Future work
Future tasks include online calibration, event camera and multi-camera expansion, dynamic scene rejection, and loop closure integration.
13

ICRA · 1991

Potential field methods and their inherent limitations for mobile robot navigation

https://doi.org/10.1109/robot.1991.131810
This paper systematically exposes the local minima, narrow passage, and oscillation problems of potential field navigation and sets the boundary conditions for future obstacle avoidance research.
01
Background
Early mobile robot navigation was widely used because the potential fields that attract targets and push away obstacles are simple and real-time.
02
Problem
The problem is to analyze whether the artificial potential field ensures safe and complete path generation in real complex obstacle environments.
03
Prior limitations
The existing explanation focused on the intuitiveness of the method and the convenience of implementation, but did not sufficiently explain why failures such as local minima and vibration occur structurally.
04
Goal
The goal is to classify the inherent limitations of the potential field method and clarify the conditions that make it difficult to use it as is for mobile robot navigation.
05
Method
The method examines representative obstacle-goal potential formulations and geometrically analyzes failure modes for each obstacle arrangement.
06
Core idea
The key idea is to show that failures arise not only from tuning problems, but also from the topology of the vector field itself and the sensor-controlled closed-loop structure.
07
Validation
Verification is a method of showing vibration near local traps, narrow passages, and obstacles through theoretical case analysis and simulation examples.
08
Results
The results presented a conclusion that although the potential field is useful for fast reactive control, it is difficult to view as complete navigation without a global planner.
09
Comparison
The comparison is a form of criticism of the simple attractive-repulsive field practices of the time, and the key is a failure taxonomy rather than a strong numerical benchmark.
10
Significance
The significance emphasized the need for follow-up research that emphasized global-local separation, such as VFH, navigation function, sampling planner, and layered planning.
11
Limitations
The limitation is that the analysis object is focused on the classical potential field and does not directly include modern optimization-based local planners or learned cost fields.
12
Future work
Future tasks include field design with integration of global planning and local obstacle avoidance, dynamic obstacles, and stability guarantee.

https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364904045564

Background
In multi-robot systems, which robot should take on which task to increase overall mission efficiency has emerged as an important issue rather than controlling individual robots.
02
Problem
The problem is to define the task allocation structure and distinguish problem difficulty and solution group when there are heterogeneous robots and multiple tasks.
03
Prior limitations
Existing studies proposed auction, market, and behavior-based coordination, but there was a lot of confusion in calling different settings by the same name.
04
Goal
The goal is to organize MRTA into a formal taxonomy to make algorithm comparison and problem definition clear.
05
Method
The method divides the relationship between robot capability and task requirements into axes such as ST-SR, ST-MR, MT-SR, and MT-MR and connects them to the optimal assignment perspective.
06
Core idea
The core idea is that MRTA is viewed as a group of resource allocation problems rather than a specific algorithm, and the constraints on the combination of the robot and the task determine the complexity.
07
Validation
Verification is a method of mapping existing MRTA literature to taxonomy and analyzing the computational characteristics of each class rather than a new robot experiment.
08
Results
The results distinguished which settings were simplified to an assignment problem and which settings required coalition formation or scheduling.
09
Comparison
The comparison is closer to a meta-analysis that compares the differences in assumptions between problem classes rather than the performance figures of individual MRTA algorithms.
10
Significance
The significance is that it is a reference point that allows multi-robot coordination papers to specify the allocation class they solve.
11
Limitations
A limitation is that it does not model modern factors such as communication uncertainty, learning-based adaptation, and human-robot teaming in detail.
12
Future work
Future tasks include dynamic tasks, partial observability, robust allocation, and a combination of market-based methods and learning-based policies.

Real-time loop closure in 2D LIDAR SLAM

03

In existing methods, loop closure search is expensive or it is difficult to balance false positives and latency in large-scale scan matching.
04
Goal
The goal is to present a system that performs highly reliable loop closure while maintaining real-time in 2D LiDAR-based SLAM.
05
Method
The method accumulates submaps with a local trajectory builder and optimizes global constraints with correlative scan matching and sparse pose adjustment.
06
Core idea
The core idea is to quickly prune loop closure candidate scan matching using branch-and-bound search to process a large search space in real time.
07
Validation
Verification was done by checking map quality and runtime in a real-world 2D mapping scenario including backpack/robot laser data.
08
Results
The results showed that a globally consistent 2D map with loop closure could be created online, and reproducibility was improved with the open implementation of Cartographer.
09
Comparison
The comparison is based on the existing grid-based scan matching and graph SLAM systems, and the strengths are complete system implementation and real-time loop closure.
10
Significance

11
Limitations

ICRA · 2008

Reciprocal Velocity Obstacles for real-time multi-agent navigation

https://doi.org/10.1109/robot.2008.4543489
RVO enables real-time multi-agent avoidance by using a velocity obstacle where each moving agent divides the responsibility for collision avoidance in half.
01
Background
In multi-robot and crowd simulations, each agent must take into account each other's future movements while simultaneously avoiding them on the fly without centralized planning.
02
Problem
The problem is to select the next collision-free speed in real time by receiving each agent's current location, speed, and neighbor information as input.
03
Prior limitations
The existing velocity obstacle assumed that the opponent maintained its speed, so mutual avoidance could oscillate or become conservative in the reciprocal setting.
04
Goal
The goal is to obtain natural and stable multi-agent collision avoidance even when all agents calculate independently.
05
Method
The method modifies the velocity obstacle reciprocally and calculates the allowable velocity space assuming that the two agents share responsibility for collision avoidance.
06
Core idea
The core idea is to reduce side-to-side sway and excessive avoidance by using symmetric reasoning, which assumes that the opponent is avoiding in the same way.
07
Validation
Verification was conducted by observing collision rate and real-time in a simulation scene where multiple agents intersect, evacuate, and move closely together.
08
Results
The results showed that many agents can move around each other smoothly without central control, and were later extended to ORCA/RVO2.
09
Comparison
The comparison is a conceptual/simulation comparison of existing VO and reactive steering, and its strength is that it creates emergent coordination with simple local rules.
10
Significance
The significance is that it laid the foundation for standard local collision avoidance techniques in crowd simulation, game AI, and multi-robot navigation.
11
Limitations
The limitation is that it is a local method, so it cannot completely resolve deadlock, narrow passages, nonholonomic dynamics, and perception uncertainty.
12
Future work
Future tasks include combining with a global planner, reflecting dynamics constraints, and integrating heterogeneous agents and social navigation preferences.
13

Deep learning for detecting robotic grasps

https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364914549607
This is an early deep learning grasp paper that showed that a deep network can predict candidates for each object based on the Cornell Grasping Dataset and RGB-D grasp rectangle expression.
01
Background
Robot grasping is a field that is difficult to generalize through manual feature and model-based exploration because both object geometry and contact stability must be considered.
02
Problem
The problem is to predict the position, direction, and width that the parallel gripper can grasp in the RGB-D image in the form of a grasp rectangle.
03
Prior limitations
Existing methods rely on hand-crafted features and object models, which limits generalization to novel objects or sensor noise.
04
Goal
The goal is to perform grasp detection on new objects by learning visual features from labeled grasp examples.
05
Method
The method learns a deep network using the RGB, depth, and surface normal channels of the Cornell dataset as input and scores candidate rectangles.
06
Core idea
The core idea is to transform grasp into a 5D rectangle detection problem and express the end-effector geometry as a supervised learning label of image coordinates.
07
Validation
Verification includes image-wise/object-wise cross-validation of the Cornell Grasping Dataset and actual grasp execution experiments on robots such as Baxter.
08
Results
The results showed a higher grasp detection accuracy than the hand-crafted baseline at the time and that the learned grasp worked in actual robots.
09
Comparison
The comparison is a method based on prior grasp detection and shallow feature, and although the baseline is classical, it was a strong starting point for RGB-D grasp learning at the time.
10
Significance
Its significance was to popularize the benchmarks and expressions followed by image-to-grasp learning studies such as GG-CNN, Dex-Net, and affordance learning.
11
Limitations
The limitation is that it is tied to a single parallel gripper and a single-object-oriented rectangle representation, which is not directly sufficient for clutter, deformable objects, and multi-finger grasp.
12
Future work
Future tasks include cluttered scenes, closed-loop grasping, larger synthetic-real datasets, and 6-DoF grasp pose prediction.

IJRR · 1991

Robot Motion Planning: A Distributed Representation Approach

https://doi.org/10.1177/027836499101000604
This is a classic paper that connects the local minima of the potential function into a graph and accelerates high-dimensional motion planning with bitmap-type distributed representation.
01
Background
Robot motion planning was a field that was less practical in high-degree-of-freedom systems because the cost of accurate geometric calculations and search exploded as the configuration space grew.
02
Problem
The paper addresses the task of finding a collision-free path while avoiding the problem of a potential function-based planner being trapped in a local minimum.
03
Prior limitations
The existing planner worked for low-degree-of-freedom examples, but was slow for high-dimensional objects and manipulators, and its local minimum escape strategy was not systematic.
04
Goal
The goal is to solve larger planning problems by storing the workspace and configuration space as distributed representations and exploring the connection graph between local minimas.
05
Method
The method combines bitmap representation, potential field, local minimum graph search, and Brownian motion-based Monte Carlo escape procedure.
06
Core idea
The key idea is to treat local minimas like nodes and discover adjacent basins with stochastic movements, instead of simply following the potential field.
07
Validation
Verification was performed through simulation experiments on rigid objects and 8, 10, and 31 DOF manipulators in 2D and 3D workspaces.
08
Results
The results showed that it was possible to solve more DOF problems significantly faster than previous path planners, but the quantitative standard benchmarks were not as rigorous as they are today.
09
Comparison
The comparison is in the nature of showing calculation speed and degree of freedom scalability compared to the exact or potential-field planner of the time, and is not a comparison with a strong modern sampling baseline.
10
Significance
The significance is that it early showed the importance of randomized planning flow and high-dimensional configuration representation that structured local-minima avoidance.
11
Limitations
Limitations are that it is highly dependent on collision geometry and potential design, and stochastic escape does not guarantee complete optimality or success time.
12
Future work
Future tasks are to strengthen theoretical guarantees and real-time performance of probabilistic search, such as probabilistic roadmap, RRT series, and optimal sampling planner.

Supersizing self-supervision: Learning to grasp from 50K tries and 700 robot hours

https://doi.org/10.1109/icra.2016.7487517
This is a large-scale robot data paper that learned a self-supervised grasp CNN with 50K grasp attempts and 700 robot hours in which the robot directly collected failures and successes.
01
Background
Robot grasping was a typical manipulation problem that lacked generalization using only human-labeled datasets due to the variety of possible touches for each object.
02
Problem
The paper addresses the problem of using the robot's trial-and-error experience as a learning signal to predict whether an RGB-D-based grasp candidate will succeed.
03
Prior limitations
Existing model-free grasp learning was prone to overfitting in unseen objects due to human label bias and small robot trial datasets.
04
Goal
The goal is to increase unknown object generalization by learning a CNN grasp predictor with a much larger self-supervised robot grasp dataset than before.
05
Method
In the method, the robot attempts to grasp, collects success or failure, reconstructs the image patch using 18-way binary classification, and performs multi-stage hard negative mining.
06
Core idea
The core idea is to grow closed-loop data of perception and action by using actual robot execution results as supervision instead of good grasp labels determined by humans.
07
Validation
Validation was evaluated with several baseline and unseen object grasping experiments after training with 50K data points and 700 robot hours.
08
Results
The results report that increasing the dataset size by about 40 times and using multi-stage training achieved state-of-the-art performance on grasping unseen objects at the time.
09
Comparison
The comparison is stronger than a hand-labeled or small-scale self-supervised grasp baseline, but it is a baseline of a different generation than today's large-scale foundation policies.
10
Significance
The significance is that it is an early large-scale robot learning example that shows that the actual execution data scale itself is a key variable that changes algorithm performance in robot manipulation.
11
Limitations
Limitations are tied to the specific gripper, tabletop setup, and patch-based discrete action formulation and do not directly address failure cause explanation or long-horizon manipulation.
12
Future work
Future tasks include combining more diverse robot embodiments, tactile feedback, closed-loop policy learning, and sim-to-real pretraining.

T-RO · 2008

iSAM: Incremental Smoothing and Mapping

https://doi.org/10.1109/tro.2008.2006706
iSAM views SLAM as a sparse smoothing problem and updates QR factorization incrementally to quickly perform exact update even in trajectories with many loops.
01
Background
SLAM requires simultaneous estimation of the robot trajectory and map, and resolving the entire problem every time a loop closure occurs destroys real-time.
02
Problem
The paper addresses the incremental SLAM problem of efficiently updating a sparse smoothing information matrix when new measurements come in.
03
Prior limitations
The existing EKF-SLAM suffers from problems with linearization and state covariance scale, and although batch smoothing is accurate, the cost of recalculating each time step is high.
04
Goal
The goal is to create a real-time SLAM solver that updates only the matrix entries changed by new factors while maintaining the accuracy of batch smoothing.
05
Method
The method incrementally updates sparse QR factorization, reduces fill-in through periodic variable reordering, and extracts the necessary marginal uncertainty.
06
Core idea
The core idea is to view the SLAM posterior as a factor graph, preserve the sparsity of the computational graph, and re-triangulate only the parts affected by new information.
07
Validation
Validation evaluated components and the entire algorithm on simulated and real-world datasets in landmark and pose-only settings.
08
Results
The results showed that it provided an efficient and accurate incremental solution even in many-loop trajectories, and later became the foundation for iSAM2 and GTSAM.
09
Comparison
The comparison presents an accuracy-computation balance compared to EKF-type filtering and batch smoothing, and by today's standards, Bayes tree-based iSAM2 is a stronger follow-up baseline.
10
Significance
Significance: We made a critical infrastructure contribution to making factor graph SLAM a standard language for real-time robot inference.
11
Limitations
Limitations include the need for periodic reordering and linearization management, and strong nonlinearity or outlier data association requires a separate robust layer.
12
Future work
Future tasks include expansion to Bayes tree, fluid relinearization, robust factor, multi-robot and semantic SLAM.
13

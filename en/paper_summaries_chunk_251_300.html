Design and Analysis of a Wearable Robotic Forearm

https://doi.org/10.1109/icra.2018.8461212
The wearable robot forearm is a human augmented robot thesis that designed and analyzed a supernumerary forearm that does not replace the human arm but gives additional abilities.
01
Background
Wearable robot augmentation has developed beyond prosthetics and exoskeletons to expand users' workspace and collaboration capabilities.
02
Problem
The paper addresses the problem of creating a lightweight robotic forearm that can be worn by a person but acts like a real assistive arm for close-range collaborative tasks.
03
Prior limitations
Existing additional arm or finger devices were focused on specific tasks, remote operations, and demonstration-based movements, making it difficult to analyze them as general-purpose collaborative agents.
04
Goal
The goal is to verify the reachability, dynamics, biomechanical loading, and autonomous collaboration potential of a functional prototype created with user-centered design.
05
Method
The method combines iterative design, wearable mounting, kinematic reach analysis, biomechanical load analysis, and pilot HRI experiments comparing autonomous and voice control.
06
Core idea
The core idea is to increase the work space by adding an independent forearm attached to the body without replicating the human arm structure, while keeping the burden on the human body within an acceptable range.
07
Validation
Validation was conducted through user studies, reach workspace calculations, biomechanical analysis of several usage scenarios, and small-scale human-robot interaction experiments.
08
Results
The results showed that the working space increased by 246% compared to the natural arm reach and that autonomous motion was more advantageous than direct voice-control in terms of time efficiency and preference.
09
Comparison
The comparison was based on natural arm reach and voice-control conditions and was more of a design feasibility comparison than a strong large-scale baseline.
10
Significance
The significance lies in redefining wearable robots not as simple assistive devices but as body-attached collaborative robots that read the user's intentions and perform joint tasks.
11
Limitations
The limitations are that the prototype and pilot scale is small, and long-term wear fatigue, safety certification, and autonomy in complex real-world tasks have not been sufficiently verified.
12
Future work
Future challenges include intent recognition, predictive control, long-term ergonomics, and real-world collaborative data-based behavior generation.

02
Problem

03
Prior limitations

04
Goal
The goal is to propose a rectangle representation that is easy to learn, yet captures the actual gripper configuration, and to streamline RGB-D grasp detection.
05
Method
The method uses the grasp rectangle on the image as a label and learns and evaluates the quality of the candidate grasp using RGB and depth features.
06
Core idea
The core idea is that by expressing grasp as a 2D rectangle, you can directly connect perception output and robot execution by including position, orientation, and jaw width all at once.
07
Validation
Verification consists of offline detection evaluation using RGB-D images of multiple objects and grasp annotation, and experiments considering the possibility of actual grasping.
08
Results
The results show that the rectangle representation creates a more complete grasp candidate than the existing low-dimensional grasp representation, and has since become a reference point for research on the Cornell Grasp Dataset series.
09
Comparison
The comparison targets traditional grasp point-based methods and learning-based candidate evaluation, but does not include comparison with a modern deep learning baseline over time.
10
Significance
The significance lies in creating an image-space grasp rectangle problem definition and evaluation culture that numerous subsequent CNN grasp detectors follow.
11
Limitations
The limitation is that the 2D image rectangle does not fully explain 6-DOF grasp, contact force, occlusion, and clutter dynamics, and the data size is small by modern standards.

Future work

ICRA · 2018

Simplified Quasi-Steady Aeromechanic Model for Flapping-Wing Robots with Passively Rotating Hinges

https://doi.org/10.1109/icra.2018.8461020
This is an aerodynamic model paper that attempts to predict the lift and drag of a small flapping-wing robot using a manual rotation hinge without directly measuring the wing angle of attack.
01
Background
Since insect-type micro-flying robots rely on small actuators and flexible hinges, a design model that quickly predicts complex abnormal aerodynamic forces is important.
02
Problem
The paper addresses the problem of predicting the lift and drag of a flapping wing with a passive rotating hinge in a form that can be calculated at the actual device design stage.
03
Prior limitations
Existing quasi-steady models require knowledge of the wing's instantaneous angle of attack or rely on complex fluid calculations, making them difficult to use for repetitive design of small robots.
04
Goal
The goal is to create a simple but experimentally valid model that can estimate the average force using only hinge elasticity, aerodynamic torque, and flapping motion.
05
Method
The method assumes that aerodynamic torque and hinge restoring torque are balanced, removes the wing rotation state, and derives a modified quasi-steady equation.
06
Core idea
The core idea is to embed the mechanical equilibrium of the manual hinge into an aerodynamic model, bypassing the angle of attack, which is difficult to measure directly, as an internal model variable.
07
Validation
Verification was performed through force measurement experiments on a centimeter-scale flapping device, and the lift measured on real hardware was compared with model predictions.
08
Results
The results showed that the measured lift showed good agreement with model predictions, showing that the simplified equation can have acceptable accuracy for design space exploration.
09
Comparison
From a comparative perspective, this model is faster than high-fidelity CFD and has lower measurement burden than traditional quasi-steady approaches, but it approximates aerodynamic phenomena more coarsely.
10
Significance
The significance is that it moves the manual hinge design of flapping robots to analytical-based parameter exploration rather than experimental trial and error.
11
Limitations
The limitation relies on torque balance and averaging assumptions, so it cannot guarantee conditions such as 3D wing deformation, strong unsteady vortex, or large hinge friction.
12
Future work
Future work is to develop a richer aerodynamic-structure coupling model that includes flexible wing deformation, unsteady vortices, and real-world flight stability at various scales.

A Singular Perturbation Approach to Control of Lightweight Flexible Manipulators

https://doi.org/10.1177/027836498800700404
This is a classic control paper that designed composite control by separating the slow rigid body motion and fast vibration dynamics of a lightweight flexible manipulator.
01
Background
Lightweight robotic arms are fast and energy efficient, but the link flexibility causes vibration, making control much more difficult than rigid manipulator control.
02
Problem
The paper addresses the trajectory control problem of stabilizing the fast vibration mode of a flexible link while following a predetermined path.
03
Prior limitations
Existing rigid-body control is difficult to directly deal with flexible dynamics where the number of inputs and output variables do not match, and vibration suppression is treated as secondary.
04
Goal
The goal is to achieve both tracking and vibration suppression by separating slow joint motion and fast elastic vibration through singular perturbation.
05
Method
The method creates dynamics using Lagrangian assumed modes and designs a composite control that combines a reduced slow controller and a fast stabilizing controller.
06
Core idea
The core idea is to use the time scale difference of flexible links as a structure for control design, dividing the complex overall system into two tractable subproblems.
07
Validation
Verification was centered on a case study and extensive simulation targeting Georgia Tech's one-link flexible arm prototype.
08
Results
The results showed that slow trajectory tracking and fast vibration stabilization can be achieved simultaneously, strengthening the theoretical framework of flexible manipulator control.
09
Comparison
From a comparative perspective, it models flexible vibration more explicitly than simple rigid compensation or mode-ignoring control, but is more sensitive to model accuracy.
10
Significance
The significance is that time scale separation and composite control became standard tools in the control of flexible arm, lightweight robot, and space manipulator.
11
Limitations
Since the limits depend on linearization, mode cutting, and small perturbation assumptions, the guarantees are weak in real environments with large deformations, friction, and strong unmodeled dynamics.
12
Future work
Future tasks include robust/adaptive control, sensor-based vibration estimation, and experimental extension to multi-link, high-degree-of-freedom flexible robots.

Validation

Results

09
Comparison
From a comparative perspective, it takes advantage of the fast DDP series among trajectory optimizers while lowering the computational burden compared to constrained nonlinear programming.
10
Significance
The significance is that it provides an optimal control tool to safely handle actuator saturation in robot whole-body control and model-based RL.
11
Limitations

12
Future work

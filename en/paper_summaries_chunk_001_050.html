IJRR · 2013

Vision meets robotics: The KITTI dataset

https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364913491297
KITTI is a dataset paper that creates a common reference point for autonomous driving perception and SLAM evaluation by providing synchronized camera, LiDAR, and GPS/IMU data from actual urban driving.
01
Background
This is a benchmark study that appeared at a time when autonomous driving and mobile robotics were going beyond laboratory scenes and required recognition, estimation, and tracking performance of real road environments.
02
Problem
The paper addresses the challenge of making intertwined driving recognition problems such as stereo, optical flow, visual odometry, and 3D object detection comparable on the same platform.
03
Prior limitations
Existing datasets often remain indoors, static scenes, single sensors, and small-scale, so they do not sufficiently reflect the dynamic objects and sensor synchronization problems of actual urban driving.
04
Goal
The goal is to provide real vehicle-based multi-sensor recordings and a standard evaluation server so that the computer vision and robotics communities can compare the same problems with the same numbers.
05
Method
The authors equipped a VW station wagon with a stereo camera, Velodyne laser scanner, and GPS/IMU and composed raw/synced data, annotations, and benchmark protocols.
06
Core idea
The core idea is to realign the research flow around the dataset by combining a real driving sensor stack and an open leaderboard rather than the algorithm itself.
07
Validation
Verification was done with several task splits and baseline results collected from Karlsruhe urban driving scenes, and corresponds to the evaluation of a dataset based on an actual robot driving platform.
08
Results
As a result, KITTI became a representative benchmark in the fields of stereo, flow, odometry, and detection, and was later repeatedly used as reference data in autonomous driving perception papers.
09
Comparison
From a comparative perspective, KITTI has stronger sensor diversity and outdoor dynamic scenes than the Middlebury-like static vision data, but has biases tied to specific cities and vehicle platforms.
10
Significance
The significance is that it significantly increases the speed of algorithm development and reproducibility by combining SLAM, 3D detection, tracking, and driving perception into one data ecosystem.
11
Limitations
The limitation is that it does not guarantee generalization and long-tail safety as it is based on German city centers, specific sensor heights and vehicle trajectories, and limited weather and road culture.
12
Future work
Future tasks are to expand the distribution of actual driving conditions by expanding to more diverse regions, bad weather, long-term repetitive driving, and semantic/interactive tasks.
13

T-RO · 2015

ORB-SLAM: A Versatile and Accurate Monocular SLAM System

https://doi.org/10.1109/tro.2015.2463671
ORB-SLAM is a paper that elevates monocular SLAM to the level of a real-time system by using ORB features uniformly throughout the entire process of tracking, mapping, relocalization, and loop closing.
01
Background
The monocular camera SLAM was an important foundational technology for mobile robots, AR, and drones as it could simultaneously perform location estimation and guidance with only a low-cost sensor.
02
Problem
The paper solves the problem of performing robust real-time SLAM on monocular video with scale ambiguity and tracking loss from small indoors to large outdoors.
03
Prior limitations
The existing PTAM series relied heavily on narrow environments and manual initialization, and was weak in integrated loop closing, relocalization, and long-term map management.
04
Goal
The goal is to create a general-purpose feature-based monocular SLAM system with automatic initialization, tracking, local mapping, loop closing, and relocalization.
05
Method
The method combines ORB features, covisibility graph, essential graph optimization, keyframe/point culling, and place recognition in parallel threads.
06
Core idea
The core idea is to use the same ORB representation in all SLAM subtasks and maintain only compact and trackable maps in a survival-of-the-fittest manner.
07
Validation
Verification focuses on offline dataset evaluation by comparing trajectory accuracy and robustness in a total of 27 sequences from several public datasets.
08
Results
The results showed low drift and strong relocalization in a wider scene than the monocular SLAM baselines at the time, and the paper improved reproducibility with open code.
09
Comparison
The targets of comparison are the major monocular SLAMs at the time, such as PTAM and LSD-SLAM, and focus on sparse feature-based accuracy and stability rather than dense reconstruction.
10
Significance
The significance is that it is a usable monocular SLAM architecture that later became a direct reference point for ORB-SLAM2/3, visual-inertial SLAM, and open-source SLAM benchmarks.
11
Limitations
Limitations include being vulnerable to monocular scale, dynamic objects, textureless scenes, rolling shutter, and illumination changes, and does not provide dense semantic maps.
12
Future work
Future tasks naturally led to stereo/RGB-D/IMU integration, multi-map lifelong operation, dynamic scene handling, and semantic robustness.
13

01
Background
Model-based control and reinforcement learning rely heavily on accurate physics simulators that can quickly evaluate many candidate controllers.
02
Problem

03
Prior limitations
Existing general-purpose game/robot engines often did not fit well with control optimization needs in spring-damper contact, joint constraint processing, and derivative approach.
04
Goal
The goal is to create a physics engine specialized for model-based control and provide XML model specification and efficient contact dynamics.
05
Method
The method combines generalized coordinate articulated dynamics, recursive computation, convex contact model, velocity-stepping solver, and MJCF/XML modeling.
06
Core idea
The key idea is to secure stability and speed even in stiff contact by releasing the contact with an optimized constraint response rather than a penalty spring.
07
Validation
Validation is a software/simulation evaluation that demonstrates simulation performance under various articulated model and contact-rich control scenarios.
08
Results
The results showed the possibility of fast rollout and stable contact processing required for control optimization, and later became one of the de facto standard engines for robot learning benchmarks.
09
Comparison
From a comparative perspective, MuJoCo emphasized a design closer to control optimization and differentiable-friendly modeling than ODE/Bullet general-purpose engines.
10
Significance
The significance is that sim-to-real, reinforcement learning, and trajectory optimization research provide a common basis for repeatedly experimenting with complex robot models.
11
Limitations
The limitation is that not all contact physics match reality, and friction/contact parameter tuning and simulator-reality gap still remain bottlenecks in policy transfer.
12
Future work
Future tasks lead to more accurate contact calibration, differentiable simulation, real-world identification, and integration of learning pipeline.

Sampling-based algorithms for optimal motion planning

This paper extends sampling-based motion planning from simple feasibility search to optimal planning with asymptotic optimality through RRT* and PRM*.
01
Background
In high-dimensional robot motion planning, exact planning was difficult, so sampling-based planners were widely used, but ensuring optimality remained a key task.
02
Problem
The paper goes beyond finding a collision-free path and solves the problem of designing a sampling-based planning algorithm whose path cost converges to the optimal solution.
03
Prior limitations
Existing PRM and RRT provided probabilistic completeness, but the guarantee that solution quality would converge to the optimal value was weak even as the number of samples increased.
04
Goal
The goal is to grant asymptotic optimality to sampling-based planning while maintaining the scalability and implementability of existing planners.
05
Method
The method proposes PRM* and RRT*, and gradually improves the path cost by theoretically setting the neighbor radius and rewiring conditions.
06
Core idea
The core idea is to make the graph recover the optimal path structure by reexamining the connections around new samples rather than simply expanding the tree/roadmap.
07
Validation
Verification was done through theoretical proof and numerical simulation, and was more of an algorithmic benchmark than an actual robot hardware experiment.
08
Results
The results show that RRT* and PRM* have both stochastic completeness and asymptotic optimality, and suggest cost improvement as the sample increases.
09
Comparison
The comparison targets are RRT and PRM, and although the new algorithm may be disadvantageous in initial speed, it has a structural advantage in long-term solution quality.
10
Significance
The significance is that it provided a theoretical starting point for the series of optimal sampling planners such as BIT*, FMT*, informed sampling, and kinodynamic optimal planning.
11
Limitations
Limitations include that convergence can be slow in high-dimensional narrow passage and kinodynamic constraints, and finite-time performance greatly depends on the problem structure.
12
Future work
Future tasks include combining admissible heuristic, batch sampling, informed set restriction, dynamic constraints, and real-time replanning.

02
Problem

03
Prior limitations
Existing methods relied on manual correspondence points, limited viewpoint relationships, and registration vulnerable to noise, making it difficult to automatically integrate complex object surfaces.
04
Goal
The goal is to accumulate multiple scans into a single surface model by repeatedly estimating the relative pose between overlapping range views.
05
Method
The method updates the rigid transformation using an iterative matching procedure that minimizes the distance between surface points and the tangent plane.
06
Core idea
The core idea is to use a point-to-plane local surface error to construct an objective function that fits the surface alignment more directly than a simple point-to-point.
07
Validation
Verification is an offline 3D reconstruction experiment targeting object scans with multiple range images, and is not centered on actual robot manipulation experiments.
08
Results
The results show that multiple range views can be progressively integrated into a coherent object model, creating an important comparative axis for subsequent ICP studies.
09
Comparison
From a comparative perspective, this method is cited as the two main bases of classical registration along with Besl-McKay ICP, but it is sensitive to initial values and local minima.
10
Significance
The significance is that it established 3D registration as a core procedure in object modeling and subsequently influenced SLAM, scanning, medical imaging, and point cloud registration.
11
Limitations
The limitation is that it assumes sufficient overlap with rigid static objects, and modern robust handling of outliers, symmetry, and large initial misalignment is limited.
12
Future work

Fast Point Feature Histograms (FPFH) for 3D registration

https://doi.org/10.1109/robot.2009.5152473
FPFH simplifies the calculation of point cloud local geometry descriptor, providing practical speed and expressiveness in 3D registration and object recognition.
01
Background
The key to 3D point cloud registration is estimation of corresponding points, and for this, a local descriptor that reliably summarizes the surrounding surface geometry was needed.
02
Problem
The paper solves the problem of creating a descriptor that can be used in large point clouds by reducing computational costs while maintaining the expressiveness of the Point Feature Histogram.
03
Prior limitations
The existing PFH calculates all neighbor pair relationships and has good descriptor quality, but its complexity is high, putting a heavy burden on the real-time registration pipeline.
04
Goal
The goal is to streamline correspondence search in 3D registration by proposing a faster local feature histogram.
05
Method
The method calculates the Simplified PFH between the query point and its neighbors and then creates an FPFH descriptor by weighted aggregation of the SPFHs of the surrounding neighbors.
06
Core idea
The core idea is to preserve local geometry statistics through a 2-stage approximation without directly computing all pairwise relationships between neighbors.
07
Validation
Verification is performed with offline experiments comparing descriptor matching and alignment performance in range scan and point cloud registration tasks.
08
Results
The results showed that the amount of calculation was greatly reduced compared to PFH, while maintaining much of the discriminative power required for registration.
09
Comparison
The objects of comparison are PFH and existing local features, and FPFH's strength is the practical trade-off between descriptor richness and runtime.
10
Significance
The significance is that it is included in PCL and is widely used as a basic feature for ICP initial alignment, object recognition, and 3D registration.
11
Limitations
Its limitations are that it is sensitive to normal estimation quality, point density, occlusion, and repetitive geometry, and has limited expressiveness compared to learned 3D descriptors.
12
Future work
Future tasks include integration with robust normal estimation, global registration, learned descriptor, and outlier rejection.
13
